We are building a decision-support tool for a water utility or municipality that needs to set differentiated water prices.
The tool should determine a recommended price per cubic metre of water based on:

- water availability / scarcity
- water quality
- quantity consumed
- user type: household or company
- for companies, pollution generated by their water use/discharge
- minimum and maximum permitted prices for each user type
- optionally, a target total revenue that the water provider wants to generate
  The objective is to balance three competing goals:

1. Keep household water affordable, while avoiding artificially low prices.
2. Increase prices when water becomes scarce or expensive to make usable.
3. Charge companies more when their use creates greater pollution or treatment costs.
   Before writing code, formalize this as a mathematical pricing and optimization model. Define every variable, parameter, constraint and objective function. Separate assumptions from values that could come from real datasets.
   Do not invent official tariffs or claim assumed parameters are real data.

Critique the proposed pricing model before implementing it.

Test whether it satisfies these expected behaviours:

- &#x20;Higher scarcity should never result in a lower price, all else equal.&#x20;
- &#x20;Lower usable water quality should increase price when additional treatment is required.&#x20;
- &#x20;Greater industrial pollution should never reduce a company's price.&#x20;
- &#x20;Household prices must remain between the household floor and ceiling.
- &#x20;Company prices must remain between the company floor and ceiling.
- &#x20;Higher consumption may trigger progressive pricing if we enable that option.
- &#x20;A revenue target must not override hard affordability ceilings.
- &#x20;If the requested revenue target is mathematically impossible under the floors and ceilings, the model must report that it is infeasible rather than silently breaking constraints.
- &#x20;Two otherwise identical users should receive the same price.

Identify contradictions, missing variables, and cases where our assumptions could produce perverse incentives. Then propose the simplest defensible formulation.
